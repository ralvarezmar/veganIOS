# Objective and striking writing

In addition to valuing objectivity and assertiveness, we also need to pay attention to the choice of verbs, to make the reading less monotonous or confusing. Some texts seem to use only half a dozen verbs that repeat and repeat, don't they?

## Strong verbs

Always choose the most precise verbs and in the imperative mood, avoid generic or imprecise verbs.

| Generic verb | Precise Verb |
| ---------- | ----------------- |
| "The error **occurs** when divided by zero" | "Dividing by zero **causes** an error" |
| "This error **happens** when you get zero" | "The system will **return** an error when it receives zero" |
| "We're careful about" | "We **take care** for that" |

Sounds hard to differentiate?

> Generic verbs define imprecision or do not make it clear who the actors (subjects) of that sentence are;
>
> Precise verbs help the reader identify the actors in the sentence and the actions that need to be taken, in an objective way.

## Remove the words "there are" or "there are" at the beginning of sentences

Starting a sentence with "There is" or "there are" makes the text monotonous, and removing them will positively alter the sentence from a generic subject to a specific subject.

Let's replace the "There's one" with "A" in the following sentence:

> **There is** a variable called 'save-exact' with the value 'true'❌
>
> **A** variable 'save-exact' has the value 'true' ✅

The sentence is objective and it is easier to find the main information.

If the sentence has the word "you" at the end, in addition to removing the **ha"/"exist**, we put the **you** at the beginning of the sentence:

> There are two things about Angular that you need to know ❌
>
> You need to know two things about Angular ✅

In scenarios where the sentence has a hidden subject, remember that this can make it difficult for those reading your text to interpret it.

> **There is no guarantee** that updates will be received ❌

Replace the **Does Not Exist** with a meaningful noun to understand the sentence. In this example, we would replace it with the noun **customers**:

> Customers are not guaranteed to receive updates ✅

## Minimize the use of unnecessary Adjectives and Adverbs

Adding imprecise adjectives like "screamingly faster" will leave unclear how "faster" something has become, as well as distracting readers.

> Use our CLI to make your build **incredibly** faster" ❌
>
> Use our CLI to make your build **2 times** faster" ✅

In the second example, you'll tell how many times the build will get faster. In addition to being more informative than an inaccurate adjective like "incredibly," substitution helps the writing have more credibility.
